STORE SIGN-IN AND MULTIPLAYER IDENTITY

The launcher opens Steam's or GOG's sign-in page. You enter your account credentials on the
provider's page. The project handles the resulting verification response or authorization code
and tokens, rather than asking you to enter your store password into a project-owned form.

The sign-in service reads the store account identifier and display name and checks game
ownership through the selected provider. The current verification code requests the account's
owned-game list to check eligibility. The multiplayer identity credential contains the provider,
account identifier, display name, and an expiry time; it does not contain the owned-game list.

The launcher caches that signed credential locally so the game can reuse your sign-in. The game
sends it to Epic Online Services. The project's identity endpoint supplies Epic with the verified
account identifier, display name, provider, and expiry. Epic supplies the game with a product user
identifier used for multiplayer connections and returning-player checks. This flow does not
require a separate Epic Games account.

MULTIPLAYER INFORMATION AND OTHER PLAYERS

Account and product user identifiers: Authentication, lobby membership, peer connections, and reserving a returning player's place in a campaign. Processed by the project and Epic; product identifiers are also used by connected game clients.
Game name, content pack and version, scenario, faction, room state, campaign identifier, and whether a password is required: Advertising and discovering games through Epic's lobby service. Treat advertised game details as visible to other players.
Display names and chat: Showing who is playing and delivering messages. General chat goes to other participants in the browser chat channel; lobby chat goes to lobby participants.
Orders, speed and battle responses, checksums, and shared game state: Running and checking the multiplayer simulation, loading saves, and reconnecting. Sent between the participating game clients through direct or relayed networking.
IP addresses and connection information: Establishing connections and delivering network traffic. Direct connections can expose your network address to other participants; service and relay providers also process connection information.

In-game chat is delivered into campaign messages and can remain in saves and reconnect
checkpoints shared with the other participant. Other players can retain information they
receive. Do not put passwords or other sensitive information into names or chat.

Agreement acceptance is saved in your local settings as the version of the agreement you accepted.
This record is separate from optional analytics consent.

LOCAL STORAGE AND TECHNICAL INFORMATION

The game and launcher store settings, cached sign-in credentials, saves, reconnect checkpoints,
and diagnostic logs on your device. Campaign data can include player names, multiplayer user
identifiers, faction assignments, and in-game messages. Checkpoints retain the information
needed to associate a returning account with its campaign.

Sign-in, update, download, and online-service requests also expose network and request information
to the services handling them. Local logs can include errors, timestamps, installation paths,
and technical details. Reports or logs you choose to send to the project may contain personal
information; review them first. Information posted to a public issue tracker is public.

OPTIONAL UNITY ANALYTICS

In builds with project analytics enabled, collection requires your separate analytics choice.
The project events describe lobby creation, campaign starts and progress, winning factions,
and local connection, reconnection, synchronization-stop, and session-closure observations.
Fields include campaign, lobby, and game-initialization identifiers; player role; selected and
winning faction; content pack and version; game settings; current turn; fixed connection-reason
categories; and territorial and military totals. Multiplayer campaign totals are reported by
the host. Connection reports describe what a client observed, rather than proving which
participant's device or network caused an interruption.

Multiplayer connection events include a project-specific pseudonymous identifier derived from
the reporting player's Epic product user identifier. This allows the project to group that
player's reports across sessions. The custom events do not send raw account identifiers,
another participant's account identifier, display names, or chat. These identifiers and
provider-generated data can still make analytics records personal data; they are not anonymous.

Unity's Analytics SDK also processes information such as IP addresses and installation-specific
identifiers, as described in its Analytics privacy overview (https://docs.unity.com/en-us/analytics/privacy-and-consent/privacy-overview).

You can change the choice using Share Analytics in the gameplay options and apply the
settings. Declining or withdrawing the choice prevents further project analytics collection.
Changing that setting does not itself request deletion of records already held by Unity.
Accepting the player agreement or using multiplayer is not analytics consent.

RETENTION AND YOUR CHOICES

Cached credentials have expiry times. Expiry prevents their further use for sign-in; it does
not necessarily remove the local files. Local saves, checkpoints, and logs remain until removed
or replaced. Removing local files does not remove another player's copies or records held by
service providers, and uninstalling may leave local data behind.

Service providers also process technical records under their own policies and service settings.
Credential expiry does not establish a deletion date for provider records, analytics, or support
correspondence. This notice does not promise automatic deletion when you uninstall.

Depending on the law that applies, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or obtain a
copy of personal information, restrict or object to processing, withdraw consent, or complain
to a supervisory authority. Contact the privacy address above for requests about
project processing. Do not send your password or sign-in tokens. Requests about a store account
or a provider's independent processing can also be made directly to that provider.
